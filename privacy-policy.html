What the app does

RecoverX is a file-recovery utility that runs on your Android device. It looks through the parts of your storage that Android allows an app to read, and lists files that appear to be recoverable: items in the system trash and in recycle-bin folders, cached thumbnails, readable app caches, leftovers from deleted files, and - during a Deep Scan - files identified by inspecting their contents rather than their names.

The kinds of file it can find and offer back to you are:

  • Photos - JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, HEIC, HEIF, AVIF, DNG and TIFF.
  • Videos - MP4, MOV, 3GP, MKV, WebM and AVI.
  • Audio - MP3, WAV, AAC, M4A, OGG, Opus, FLAC, AMR and MIDI.
  • Documents - PDF, plain text, CSV, RTF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OpenDocument files.
  • Archives - ZIP, RAR, 7z, GZIP and TAR.

Anything it cannot place in one of those groups is listed as "Other" rather than guessed at.

When you choose items to recover, the app writes fresh copies into folders it creates for you: Pictures/RecoverX, Movies/RecoverX, Music/RecoverX and Documents/RecoverX. Archives and unrecognised files are filed with the documents. Originals are never modified, moved or deleted.

There is one exception, and it is not ours to carry out. For an item still in the Android system trash, RecoverX can ask Android to restore it in place. Android - not the app - then shows you its own confirmation dialog, and only you can approve it. RecoverX cannot bypass that dialog and never deletes anything.

Permissions, and why each one is asked for

RecoverX asks for the access it needs to search and to save what it finds, and nothing beyond that. Every one of these can be refused; the app keeps working and tells you which sources it could not search as a result.

Photos and videos (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO)
Reads image and video files so they can be examined and offered back to you. This is the core of what the app does.
Selected photos and videos only (READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED)
On Android 14 and later you may grant access to specific items instead of your whole library. When you do, the app searches only those items and says on the home screen that its access is limited.
Music and audio (READ_MEDIA_AUDIO)
Optional. Lets the app find recoverable audio files. Refusing it means the audio source is reported as unavailable rather than searched.
Files and media on older Android (READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE)
The equivalent of the above on Android 12L and earlier, which had no per-type media permissions.
Saving files on older Android (WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE)
Requested only on Android 9 and earlier, which required it to write recovered copies into shared storage.
A folder you choose
You can point Deep Scan at one specific folder. Android grants the app read access to that folder only, and it stays granted until you clear it or uninstall the app.
All files access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE)
Optional, and the app never asks you for it - not at startup and not anywhere in the interface. It is declared because some recoverable leftovers are invisible to an app without it, so if you have granted it yourself in Android Settings, Deep Scan uses the wider view it gives. Deep Scan works without it, over a smaller area.
Internet (INTERNET) and network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE)
Used only to request and display ads. No file, file name, path or preview is ever sent over the network.
Advertising ID (AD_ID)
Declared so the Google AdMob SDK can use your device's advertising identifier. See the advertising section below.

A few more appear on the installed app that RecoverX itself does not ask for. They are added automatically by the Google Mobile Ads SDK and the Android libraries it brings with it, and they are listed here so that the permission list you can inspect on your device has nothing in it this policy has not accounted for:

ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS
Android's Privacy Sandbox interfaces, used by Google's advertising code. No RecoverX code calls them.
WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE
Come from Google Play services components and the Android WorkManager library that the ads SDK depends on. RecoverX schedules no background work of its own; it does nothing at all while you are not using it.

How your files are handled

  • Scanning, identifying, previewing and copying all happen on this device, inside the app.
  • To tell a photo from a document, and one copy of a file from another, the app reads a small amount of each file's contents - a header and a sample from each end. Those bytes are used to classify and de-duplicate, and are then discarded.
  • Previews are generated on the device and held in memory only. They are cleared when a new scan starts and when the app closes.
  • The list of recoverable items exists only while the app is running. It is not written to disk and does not survive closing the app.
  • Nothing about your files - not the content, not the names, not the paths, not the previews - is transmitted off the device.

Please note that files RecoverX recovers into Pictures/RecoverX and the other RecoverX folders are ordinary files in your shared storage. Like any other photo on your phone, they are visible to other apps you have granted media access to, and they appear in your gallery. Delete them yourself if you would rather they were not there.

What the app stores on your device

Two settings, and nothing else. Neither describes you or your files:

theme_mode
Whether you chose the light, dark or system theme.
last_interstitial_at
When a full-screen ad was last shown, so the app can leave a decent gap before the next one.

These stay on the device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes them. There is no account, no identifier of our own and no database of your files: the list of recoverable items is held in memory only and is gone when the app closes.

One more thing persists, and Android holds it rather than the app. If you point Deep Scan at a folder, Android records that you granted read access to that one folder and keeps the grant across restarts. Clearing it in the app, or uninstalling the app, hands it back.

Released builds of RecoverX write no diagnostic logs at all. In development builds the app logs to help with debugging, and even then file paths are reduced to their shape - the number of folders deep and the file extension - so that a log line cannot name your photo. No log is ever sent anywhere.

Advertising: Google AdMob

RecoverX is free and is paid for by advertising. It uses Google AdMob, and only Google AdMob, to show:

  • a banner at the bottom of the home screen and the results screen;
  • ads that sit in the page alongside the app's own content, on the home screen between the two scan buttons and on the results screen;
  • one full-screen ad when the app opens;
  • a video ad before each Quick Scan and Deep Scan, which is what pays for the scan you are about to run;
  • while a scan is actually running, up to three full-screen ads, at roughly 40, 80 and 120 seconds in. A scan that finishes or is cancelled sooner shows fewer, or none at all, and no scan ever shows more than three.

No ad is shown while files are being recovered - that part of the app is never interrupted - and none is shown at the moment a scan finishes, so your results appear as soon as they are ready.

Because a scan is preceded by a video ad, starting one needs a working internet connection. If the ad cannot be loaded the app says so and the scan does not start. Nothing else in the app requires a connection: files already found can still be recovered offline, and an ad that fails to load during a scan is simply skipped without affecting the scan.

To do this, Google's advertising code runs inside the app and communicates with Google's servers. Google collects and processes data through it - which may include your device's advertising identifier, information about your device, your IP address, and how you interact with the ads it shows. Google acts on its own behalf for this, and what it collects and why is governed by Google's own policies rather than by us:

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • About AdMob and advertising ID: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543

We do not receive that data. What Google shows us is aggregated reporting - how many ads were shown and what they earned - which does not identify you or anyone else.

Nothing about you is recorded when you watch the video before a scan. The app keeps no count of ads you have seen beyond the single timestamp named under "What the app stores on your device", and no record of which ads you were shown.

Your advertising choices

RecoverX does not currently show a consent or privacy-options prompt of its own, and it has no in-app setting that turns ads off or switches personalised ads to non-personalised ones. We would rather say that plainly than describe a control that is not there.

The controls that do exist are on your device and in your Google account, and they work whether or not an app offers its own:

  • Reset or delete your advertising ID, and switch off ad personalisation, in Android Settings - on most devices under Privacy, then Ads. Doing either changes nothing about what RecoverX can find or recover.
  • Review and change what Google uses to personalise ads at https://myadcenter.google.com.
  • Read what Google collects through AdMob, and why, at the links above.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, note that this version of the app does not present a consent message before ads are requested. If that changes, this policy and the version number at the top will change with it.

Third parties in this app

One third party receives anything at all, and it is named in full above:

Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK)
Requests and displays the ads, and communicates with Google's servers to do it. This is the only component in the app that uses the network.

That SDK does not arrive alone. It brings Google Play services components and Android libraries with it - for the advertising identifier, for drawing ads in a web view, and for scheduling its own background work. They are part of Google's advertising code rather than separate services, and what Google does with what they collect is covered by the Google links above.

The rest of the app is built with other people's code as every app is - the Flutter framework, a settings-storage plugin, a video player for previewing your own files, and Android's own support libraries. None of those contacts a server, and none is a service that receives your information. They are listed here only so that "third parties" is not left vague.

We have added no analytics service, no crash-reporting service, no attribution SDK, no advertising SDK other than Google's and no social-network SDK, and there is no backend of ours: nothing in this app talks to a server we control, because there is not one. We receive no usage measurement from the app at all.

What RecoverX does not do

  • It has no accounts, no sign-in and no profile. We never ask for your name, email address, phone number or contacts.
  • It carries no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK of ours, and reports no usage measurement to us. We do not know how you use the app.
  • It does not use your device's location, and it does not read the location recorded inside your photos. It holds no permission that would let it: Android strips that information out of the photos it hands the app, and RecoverX never asks for the access that would undo that.
  • It does not read your messages, your call history, your contacts or your calendar, and asks for no permission that would let it.
  • We do not sell or share your personal information, and we hold none to sell.

Keeping and deleting data

Because we operate no server and receive none of your content, there is nothing held by us to request a copy of or ask us to delete. Everything is on your device and under your control:

  • Withdraw any permission at any time in Android Settings, under Apps, then RecoverX, then Permissions.
  • Clear a folder you granted from within the app, which hands the access back.
  • Delete recovered files the way you would delete any other file.
  • Clear the app's data, or uninstall it, to remove the two settings above.

Data Google holds from serving ads is subject to Google's policies and your Google account controls, linked under "Advertising: Google AdMob" and "Your advertising choices".

Children

RecoverX is a general-audience file-recovery utility, intended for the owner of the device. It is not directed at children, it is not built for a children's audience, and we do not knowingly collect anything from a child - we collect nothing from anyone. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please write to the contact address at the end of this policy.

The ads are chosen by Google rather than by us, and the app does not restrict them to a particular content rating. That is worth knowing if you hand the device to a child.

Security

  • The strongest protection here is structural: your files never leave the device, so there is no transfer to intercept and no database of ours to breach.
  • The app is configured to refuse unencrypted network connections.
  • Diagnostic logging is compiled out of released builds entirely.

No app can promise perfect security, and this one does not. What it can promise is that it does not create the risk in the first place by moving your files off your phone.
